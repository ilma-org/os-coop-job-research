# Education and qualifications

## Summary

The SRE book describes Google's SRE hiring as two groups: 50-60% are people hired via the standard Google Software Engineer procedure, and the other 40-50% are candidates very close to those qualifications who also have rare technical skills useful to SRE (01-57, 01-58). UNIX system internals and networking (Layer 1 to Layer 3) are named as the two most common of those alternate skills (01-59). The books describe a mix of software development and systems engineering backgrounds, and say what helps new SREs learn: shadowing on-call, nontrivial project work, reverse engineering and a checklist of focus areas (01-63, 01-65, 01-66, 01-67, 01-107). One Google job ad, for Software Engineer, Android SRE, lists a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science, a related field, or equivalent practical experience as a minimum, with a Master's degree preferred (01-71, 01-73). The books themselves do not give a degree requirement in the passages read.

## Key points

- Hiring mix: 50-60% of SREs are Google Software Engineers hired via the standard procedure; the other 40-50% are candidates who were very close to those qualifications (85-99% of the skill set required) and had additional technical skills rare for most software engineers. (01-57, 01-58)
- Alternate skills sought: UNIX system internals and networking (Layer 1 to Layer 3) are the two most common. (01-59)
- Common traits: belief in and aptitude for developing software systems to solve complex problems; SREs share academic and intellectual background with the rest of the development organization. (01-69, 01-60)
- Background mix: Google hires people with a diverse background in systems and software engineering, and strives for a mix of traditional software development and systems engineering experience on SRE teams. (01-62, 01-63)
- Performance: Google tracks the career progress of both groups closely and found no practical difference in performance between the two tracks. (01-108)
- Learning the role: investing up front in the education and technical orientation of new SREs makes them better engineers. (01-64)
- Practices the SRE book recommends for new SREs: shadow the on-call rotation early, take on nontrivial project work with partial ownership in the stack, and use reverse engineering, statistical thinking and working from fundamental principles. (01-65, 01-66, 01-67)
- Pre-on-call checklist: in a Workbook case study, a new Google SRE team compiled a checklist of two dozen focus areas to practice before going on-call. (01-107)
- Job ad, minimum qualifications (one ad, Software Engineer, Android SRE): a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science, a related field, or equivalent practical experience; 2 years of experience with software development in one or more programming languages. (01-71, 01-72)
- Job ad, preferred qualifications (same ad): a Master's degree in Computer Science or Engineering; 2 years of experience designing, analyzing, and troubleshooting large-scale distributed systems. (01-73, 01-74)

## Sources and limits

Used SRE book chapters 1, 11, 18 and 28 and Workbook chapter 8, all from https://sre.google/books/, plus one Google Careers job ad (archived 2026-10-11). The books describe how Google hires and trains SREs; the passages read do not list degrees, certificates or portfolio requirements, and this note does not infer any. The job-ad claims come from a single ad, so they show one posting's wording, not a rule for every SRE role or level. The SRE book footer says copyright 2017 and the Workbook footer says copyright 2018, so each claim states what the book says, not necessarily current practice. Quotes are kept short; open the source for the full passage.
